# SHEH YouTube — 5 Video Outlines

Founder-to-camera videos, all filmable on an iPhone. No studio, no
editing tricks needed — steady phone, decent light, honest talk.
That IS the brand.

**General rules for every video:**
- Talk like you talk. One take is fine. Don't read a script — glance at
  the talking points and say it your way.
- Film horizontal (landscape) for YouTube.
- End every video the same way: "Start hard today, be harder than
  yesterday, for today is yesterday's tomorrow." Make it the channel's
  signature sign-off.
- Never make claims about nutrition or health benefits of the bars.
  Flavors and the story are fair game; medical claims are not.

---

## Video 1 — The Origin Story

**Title:** Two Friends, Full-Time Jobs, and a Protein Bar Company — Why We Started SHEH
**Thumbnail text:** WHY WE STARTED
**Length:** 4–6 minutes. Sit at the kitchen table where the batches get made.

**Hook (first 15 seconds):**
"We have full-time jobs. We have responsibilities. And we started a
protein bar company anyway. Here's why."

**Talking points:**
1. Who you are — two friends, day jobs, real lives. No investors, no
   playbook. (Add the true details: how you met, whose kitchen,
   when the idea hit.)
2. The moment it started — tell the real story, whatever it is. Specific
   beats polished.
3. What SHEH means: Start Hard, End Hard — and why that's the company,
   not just the name.
4. Where it stands today: small batches, first bars on the way, building
   it before work and after bedtime.
5. The invite: "Follow along. We're going to show you the whole climb —
   including the parts that go wrong."

## Video 2 — The Philosophy

**Title:** What "Start Hard, End Hard" Actually Means
**Thumbnail text:** START HARD. END HARD.
**Length:** 3–5 minutes. Film outside if you can — trail, driveway at dawn, anywhere real.

**Hook:**
"Everybody's selling you an easier life. We named our company after a
harder one. On purpose."

**Talking points:**
1. The premise: life is hard whether you like it or not — early alarms,
   kids, work, responsibility. You don't get a vote on hard. You get a
   vote on what kind of man meets it.
2. Start hard: win the first hour and the day follows.
3. End hard: finish what you start — the last rep, the bedtime story
   when you're wrecked, the promise you keep when nobody would notice
   if you didn't.
4. What this is NOT: not yelling, not shame, not pretending hard means
   miserable. A steady man, not a loud one.
5. Sign-off with the quote.

## Video 3 — Making a Batch

**Title:** Making a Batch of SHEH Bars in Our Kitchen (the Real Operation)
**Thumbnail text:** SMALL BATCH. BIG STANDARD.
**Length:** 5–8 minutes. Mix of talking and b-roll: hands, bowls, trays, the mess.

**Hook:**
"No factory. No co-packer. This is the entire SHEH production line —
and tonight it makes a batch."

**Talking points:**
1. The setup: when batches happen (after work? weekends?) and what a
   batch night actually looks like.
2. Walk through the process at a high level — weighing, mixing, pressing,
   cutting. **Keep the exact recipe and amounts off camera.**
3. What "small batch" buys you: control, care, the ability to throw out
   anything that isn't right.
4. The unglamorous truth: cleanup, trial and error, batches that failed.
   People trust what they can see.
5. Tease: "Want to try one when the first batch ships? Link below —
   email list hears first."

## Video 4 — The 5 AM Routine

**Title:** A Real 5 AM Routine — No Hacks, No Ice Baths, Just the Work
**Thumbnail text:** 5 AM. NO HACKS.
**Length:** 4–6 minutes. Film it live in the actual morning dark — alarm, kitchen, gym or garage.

**Hook:**
"This is not a productivity video. There's no journaling ritual and no
$40 supplement stack. This is just what getting up looks like when you
mean it."

**Talking points:**
1. The alarm moment — the honest version. It's not easy for you either.
   That's the point.
2. Your actual routine, plain: what you really do, in order. Don't
   dress it up.
3. Why mornings: it's the only ground nobody else is fighting you for —
   before the job, the kids, the phone.
4. The 80% rule: some mornings are bad. You go anyway. Consistency
   beats intensity.
5. Challenge to the viewer: "Tomorrow, get up 30 minutes earlier and do
   one hard thing before anyone needs you. Comment what it was."

## Video 5 — Hard Things With Your Kids

**Title:** Doing Hard Things With Your Kids (Why We Take Them on the Climb)
**Thumbnail text:** TAKE THEM WITH YOU
**Length:** 4–6 minutes. Ideal: filmed on a hike or after one. Kids can appear or not — your call as a parent; the talk works either way.

**Hook:**
"My kids don't listen to my speeches. They watch what I do. So we do
hard things together."

**Talking points:**
1. Kids learn hard from watching you meet it — calm, no complaints, no
   drama.
2. What "hard things together" looks like in real life: the full hike on
   small legs, finishing the chore, sticking out the practice they
   wanted to quit.
3. Let them struggle a little. Don't carry them past every steep part —
   walk next to them through it.
4. The payoff isn't the summit. It's the kid who learns "I can do hard
   things" before the world makes them prove it.
5. Sign-off with the quote — and an ask: "If you take your kids on a
   climb this weekend, tell us in the comments."

---

*Posting order suggestion: 1 → 2 → 4 → 3 → 5. Origin first so new
viewers know who you are; philosophy second; routine third because it's
the most shareable; batch video once there's an audience to tease;
fatherhood when you've got footage from a real outing.*
